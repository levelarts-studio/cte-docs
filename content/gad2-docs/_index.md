## Overview

Year 3 is where everything comes together. You'll import your Year 2 assets into **Unreal Engine**, master professional lighting and rendering, add visual effects, and build a portfolio-ready showcase. This year focuses entirely on the **production pipeline** and **career readiness**—preparing you for industry work or further education in game art and design.

## Primary Learning Objective

By the end of Year 3, students will have:
- **A fully integrated scene in Unreal Engine** with professional lighting, materials, and VFX
- **High-resolution portfolio renders** ready for presentations and job applications
- **A professional web-based portfolio** showcasing the complete capstone project
- **Career materials** including designer's statement, resume, and AVG career exploration
- **Understanding of production workflows** used in professional game and animation studios

## Core Software & Tools

- **Unreal Engine** - Real-time rendering, lighting, and visual effects
- **Blender** - Final asset adjustments and export refinement
- **Canva** - Portfolio website design and presentation materials
- **Adobe Creative Suite** (optional) - Advanced compositing and post-processing

## Prerequisites — What You Should Know

Before starting Year 3, you should have completed Year 2 and have:

- **Complete 3D Assets** — Character, props, and environment modeled and textured
- **Blender Proficiency** — Comfortable with modeling, UV mapping, and export workflows
- **Clean Geometry** — Assets ready for real-time rendering (good topology, optimized poly counts)
- **Export Pipeline Knowledge** — Understanding of FBX/OBJ exports and file preparation
- **Organized Project Files** — All assets properly named and structured

If any assets from Year 2 are incomplete or need refinement, address those issues before importing into Unreal. **Year 3 is about polish and presentation, not starting from scratch.**

## What We Cover

### Units 1-2: Importing & Lighting in Unreal
**Focus:** Exporting assets from Blender and importing into Unreal Engine; mastering lighting and color

**You will learn:**
- Proper export settings from Blender to Unreal
- Unreal Engine project setup and organization
- Importing meshes, materials, and textures
- Material setup in Unreal (PBR workflows)
- Lighting fundamentals: directional, point, spot, and area lights
- Light baking vs. real-time lighting
- Color grading and post-process volumes (AVG.17.8)
- Rendering high-quality screenshots and renders (4.5)
- Camera setup and composition in Unreal

**Standards:** 4.5 Technology (Rendering/Exporting); AVG.17.8 Skill Building; AVG.18.6 Process and Practice

### Units 3-4: Final Polish & VFX
**Focus:** Adding visual effects and working in simulated professional production environment

**You will learn:**
- Particle systems in Unreal (Niagara)
- Atmospheric effects: fog, dust, volumetric lighting
- Post-processing effects: bloom, depth of field, motion blur
- Basic compositing techniques
- Optimization for real-time rendering
- Simulated professional workflows (16.7): task tracking, deadlines, quality standards
- Completing final project deliverable (AVG.18.5)
- Production polish: details that elevate presentation quality
- Advanced materials and shader techniques (AVG.17.7, 17.9)

**Standards:** AVG.17.7, AVG.17.9 Skill Building; 16.7 Production/Project Management; AVG.18.5 Process and Practice

### Units 5-6: Portfolio & Career Readiness
**Focus:** Building professional portfolio and exploring AVG career pathways

**You will learn:**
- Finalizing high-resolution renders (4.5)
- Screenshot best practices and portfolio presentation
- Building a web-based portfolio using Canva (3.9)
- Portfolio structure: project showcase, process documentation, artist statement
- Writing a compelling designer's statement (2.7)
- Resume and cover letter for creative industries
- Understanding AVG career pathways (AVG.19.1-19.5):
  - Concept Artist
  - 3D Character/Environment Artist
  - Technical Artist
  - Lighting Artist
  - Game Designer
  - Visual Effects Artist
- Industry expectations and entry-level job requirements
- Networking, online presence, and career planning
- Preparing for internships, college programs, or entry-level positions

**Standards:** 3.9 Career Planning; 2.7 Communication; AVG.19.1-19.5 Career Exploration; 4.5 Technology

## Semester Projects

### Project 1 (End of Unit 3)
**Fully Lit and Rendered Scene in Unreal Engine with VFX**

- Visual effects integrated (particles, fog, post-processing)

- Polished final renders of the complete scene

  - Hero image and project overview
  - Process documentation (Year 1 concepts → Year 2 models → Year 3 final)

  - Designer's statement and career goals

## Teaching Philosophy: Professional Production Simulation

Year 3 simulates a **professional production environment**:
- **Milestones and deadlines** mirror industry standards
- **Quality benchmarks** reflect professional expectations
- **Feedback and iteration** match studio critique processes
- **Portfolio presentation** follows industry best practices

You'll work with the same level of rigor expected in game studios, animation houses, and creative agencies.

## Three-Year Journey Complete

By the end of Year 3, you will have taken a single project through the **entire production pipeline**:
- **Year 1:** Concept and pre-production (what it will be)
- **Year 2:** Asset creation and modeling (building it in 3D)
- **Year 3:** Integration and presentation (making it look professional)

This comprehensive portfolio piece demonstrates your ability to complete a project from start to finish—a critical skill for any creative career.

## Assessment & Skill Development

Students are assessed on:
- **Skill Building (AVG.17.7, 17.8, 17.9):** Lighting, rendering, VFX proficiency
- **Process & Practice (AVG.18.5, 18.6):** Production workflows and professional standards
- **Production/Project Management (16.7):** Meeting deadlines, task tracking, quality control
- **Technology (4.5):** Rendering, exporting, and presentation skills
- **Career Planning (3.9):** Portfolio quality, career materials, professional readiness
- **Communication (2.7):** Designer's statement, project documentation, presentation
- **Career Exploration (AVG.19.1-19.5):** Understanding of AVG pathways and career opportunities
- **Final Presentation Quality:** Polish, attention to detail, professional standards
