## The task

Block out three recognizable props using **only** transformed primitives. Move, rotate, and scale. No edit mode, no extruding, no loop cuts, no added geometry.

Then prove they read: each prop has to be recognizable in silhouette, as a solid shape with no detail.

## Before you start

Read [Primitive Modeling](/learn/blender/primitive-modeling/) (`BLND-103`).

## Steps

{{% steps %}}

### Step 1 — Pick three props that share a setting

Pick three props that share a setting, so the three read as one scene:

- **Campsite**: lantern, folding chair, cooler
- **Workshop**: vise, toolbox, work lamp
- **Kitchen**: stand mixer, kettle, stack of bowls
- **Arcade**: cabinet, stool, trash can

Choose objects with distinct outlines. A cardboard box is a cube and teaches you nothing.

### Step 2 — Gather reference

Find two or three photos of each prop from different angles. Look at proportion before detail. How tall is it compared to its width? Where does the handle sit?

### Step 3 — Thumbnail the shapes

Before opening Blender, sketch each prop as three to six simple shapes. Paper is fine. Five minutes here saves an hour later.

### Step 4 — Block out

Object mode only, no edit mode for any reason. Use as few objects as you can while keeping it recognizable. Set vertex counts when you add each primitive, not after. Get proportions right before anything else.

If you want to cut into a shape, you are past this exercise. Solve it with placement.

### Step 5 — Silhouette test

Apply a flat black material, or set the viewport to a flat shading mode, and look at each prop head-on.

If you cannot tell what it is from the outline alone, it is not done. Go back and fix proportions or arrangement. This step is the actual skill.

### Step 6 — Compose and render

Arrange all three in one scene as a small still life. Think about spacing and overlap rather than lining them up in a row. Set a camera, add basic lighting, render one image.

### Step 7 — Post

Post to your portfolio's **Coursework** page using the standard write-up format, plus:

- Your final render
- Your silhouette test image
- Your reference or thumbnails
- One sentence on which prop was hardest to make readable and what you changed

## Rubric

| Criteria | Approaching | Proficient | Advanced |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primitive Assembly (No Edit Mode)** | Edit mode tools used (extrude, cuts, booleans) | Exclusively built from transformed primitives (move, scale, rotate) in Object Mode | Efficient primitive count; clean hierarchy and parented components |
| **Silhouette & Readability** | Props indistinguishable or ambiguous in solid black silhouette | All three props instantly recognizable in flat silhouette view | Strong contrast in primary shapes; silhouettes have distinct profiles and clear negative space |
| **Proportion & Reference** | Disproportionate elements; no clear relationship to reference | Proportions closely match reference photos gathered in pre-production | Nuanced scale relationships between all three props in the scene |
| **Composition & Lighting** | Objects placed randomly or lined up flat; blown out or pitch black render | Cohesive still life arrangement with camera framing and readable lighting | Deliberate depth, overlap, focal point, and atmospheric mood in the final render |
| **Documentation & Process** | Missing thumbnails, reference, or silhouette proof image | Coursework post includes reference/thumbnails, silhouette proof, final render, and reflection | Thorough reflection detailing specific proportion adjustments made to solve silhouette readability |

## Notes

- **Silhouette is why this exercise exists**: If the outline does not read, no amount of texture will save it. Character artists test the same way.
- **The primitives-only rule is temporary**: Edit mode comes next unit. Working without it now forces you to solve with proportion and placement, which keeps paying off long after you can cut geometry.
