  Assignment 2.4 — Developing the Narrative Arc
</div>

A strong narrative arc gives your story structure and momentum. This week, you'll map out the complete narrative journey for your capstone project.

## Classic Narrative Structure

Most stories follow a pattern:
1. **Setup/Exposition**: Introduce world, characters, normal life
2. **Inciting Incident**: Event that disrupts normalcy and begins the conflict
3. **Rising Action**: Challenges escalate, stakes increase
4. **Climax**: Peak of tension, decisive moment or confrontation
5. **Falling Action**: Immediate consequences of the climax
6. **Resolution**: New normal, character growth, loose ends tied up

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Story Outline

Create a structured outline of your narrative arc using the elements above. For each stage, write 2-3 sentences describing what happens.

### Part 2: Key Scenes

Identify and describe **3-5 key scenes** that are crucial to your narrative. For each scene:
- Where does it take place?
- Who is present?
- What happens?
- Why is this scene important?

### Part 3: The Scene You'll Depict

Choose ONE scene from your narrative that you will visually depict for your capstone project. Write 200-250 words explaining:
- Why you chose this scene
- What it reveals about your world and characters
- How it represents the core of your story
- What makes it visually interesting

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
The scene you choose will be what you model in 3D (Year 2) and render in Unreal (Year 3). Choose something meaningful but achievable!
{{< /callout >}}

## What to Turn In

Submit to Canvas:
1. Full narrative arc outline (6 stages)
2. 3-5 key scene descriptions
3. Chosen scene explanation (200-250 words)

## Grading Criteria

- **Narrative Arc** (40%): Complete, follows structure, makes sense
- **Key Scenes** (30%): Well-chosen, clearly described
- **Chosen Scene** (30%): Strong justification and practical choice

**Standards Addressed**: 2.7 (Writing), 10.3 (Generating ideas), 12.1 (Audience), 1.4 (Academic skills)
