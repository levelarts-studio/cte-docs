<div class="hx:mt-1 hx:text-gray-500 hx:text-lg">
  Assignment 2.3 — Defining Central Conflict
</div>

Every compelling story needs conflict—the problem or tension that drives the narrative forward. This week, you'll identify the central conflict of your capstone project.

## Types of Conflict

- **Character vs. Character**: Personal rivalries or opposing goals
- **Character vs. Self**: Internal struggles, doubts, or moral dilemmas
- **Character vs. Society**: Fighting against cultural norms or systems
- **Character vs. Nature**: Survival against environmental challenges
- **Character vs. Technology/Unknown**: Facing forces beyond understanding

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Identify Your Central Conflict

Write a clear statement (2-3 sentences) defining the primary conflict in your story. Use the formula:
- **[Character]** wants **[goal]** but **[obstacle]** stands in the way.

### Part 2: Conflict Development

Expand on your conflict (300-400 words) by exploring:
- What created this conflict? (History, events, circumstances)
- Why does it matter? (Stakes—what happens if it's not resolved?)
- Who or what opposes the protagonist?
- What makes resolution difficult?

### Part 3: Visual Representation

Create a simple diagram or flowchart showing:
- The conflict source
- Key opposing forces or characters
- Potential paths to resolution

This can be hand-drawn or digital.

## What to Turn In

Submit to Canvas:
1. Central conflict statement (2-3 sentences)
2. Conflict development essay (300-400 words)
3. Visual diagram (photo or digital file)

## Grading Criteria

- **Conflict Statement** (25%): Clear, specific, compelling
- **Development** (50%): Detailed exploration showing critical thinking
- **Visual Diagram** (25%): Organized and easy to understand

**Standards Addressed**: 2.7 (Writing), 10.3 (Generating ideas), 1.4 (Academic skills)
