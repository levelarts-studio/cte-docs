  Concept & Narrative II: Character, Story & Conflict
</div>

In Unit 2, you'll build on your world foundation by developing the **characters** and **narrative** that inhabit it. You'll create character backstories, define motivations, establish the central conflict, and develop the narrative arc for your capstone project.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
This unit focuses on **character development** and **narrative structure**. By the end, your Concept Document will include fully developed characters and a clear story framework.
{{< /callout >}}

## Topics Covered
- Character development: creating compelling backstories and motivations
- Developing cultures and societies within your world
- Identifying central conflicts and dramatic tension
- Narrative structure: setup, conflict, resolution
- Refining and expanding your Concept Document
- Character relationships and dynamics

## Learning Report

**Objectives**
- Create detailed character backstories with clear motivations
- Develop cultural and societal elements for your world
- Identify and articulate the central conflict of your narrative
- Structure a complete narrative arc (setup, conflict, resolution)
- Compile all narrative elements into a cohesive Concept Document

**Materials**
- Google Docs (Concept Document)
- Research materials for inspiration
- Notebook for brainstorming
- Classroom computers with internet access

**Assessment**
Students demonstrate narrative development through character profiles, conflict analysis, and story structure. Final assessment is the completed Concept Document showing integration of character, world, and narrative elements.

**Activities**
Students develop character profiles with detailed backstories, research cultural development principles, identify conflict sources and tensions, map out narrative structure, and integrate all elements into their living Concept Document.

## Connection to Visual Development

The characters and narrative you develop in Unit 2 will directly inform:
- **Character designs** you'll create in Units 3-4 (what do they look like?)
- **Props and assets** related to the story (what objects matter?)
- **Scene composition** in Units 5-6 (what moment in the story will you depict?)
- **3D modeling choices** in Year 2 (bringing these characters to life)

Strong character and narrative development makes visual design decisions much clearer.
