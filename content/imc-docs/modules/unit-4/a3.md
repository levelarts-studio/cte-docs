  Assignment 4.3 — Introduction to Canva & Mood Boards
</div>

This week, you'll learn **Canva** (free web-based design tool) and create a mood board that captures your project's visual aesthetic.

## What is a Mood Board?

A mood board is a curated collection of images, colors, textures, and typography that communicate the "feel" of a project. Designers use mood boards to:
- Establish visual direction
- Communicate ideas to teams
- Ensure consistency across a project

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Learn Canva

Create a free Canva account and complete these tasks:
- Explore the interface and templates
- Practice adding images, text, and shapes
- Learn basic layout and design tools
- Create a simple practice poster (any topic)

Submit 2-3 screenshots showing your Canva exploration.

### Part 2: Gather References

Collect 15-20 reference images that capture your project's aesthetic:
- Environments similar to yours
- Character designs with similar style/mood
- Color palettes and lighting examples
- Textures and materials
- Overall mood/atmosphere references

Save these images to use in your mood board.

### Part 3: Create Mood Board in Canva

Using Canva, create a professional mood board (1920x1080px or similar):
- Organize your reference images effectively
- Include your color palettes from A4.2
- Add text labels for key themes or concepts
- Create clear visual hierarchy
- Make it look polished and professional

### Part 4: Mood Board Explanation

Write 150-200 words explaining:
- What aesthetic/mood you're trying to capture
- How the references support your vision
- What makes this collection cohesive

## What to Turn In

1. Canva exploration screenshots (2-3 images)
2. Mood board created in Canva (PDF or image export)
3. Explanation (150-200 words)

## Grading Criteria

- **Canva Proficiency** (20%): Understanding of tool
- **Mood Board** (50%): Well-curated, cohesive, professional
- **Explanation** (30%): Clear articulation of visual direction

**Standards Addressed**: 4.2 (Communication technologies), 10.2 (Research and inspiration), 12.1 (Audience consideration)
