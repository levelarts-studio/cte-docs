  Assignment 1.4 — Target Audience Analysis
</div>

Who is your project for? Understanding your **target audience** is crucial for making design decisions that resonate. This week, you'll identify and analyze who you're creating for.

## Why Audience Matters

Your target audience influences:
- **Tone and mood**: Playful vs. serious, lighthearted vs. dark
- **Complexity**: Simple vs. intricate design
- **Cultural references**: What will they recognize and connect with?
- **Art style**: Realistic, stylized, minimalist, detailed

Professional game artists and designers always design with their audience in mind—not just for themselves.

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Define Your Target Audience

Answer these questions about your intended audience:

1. **Age Range**: Who is this for? (Kids, teens, young adults, adults, all ages)
2. **Experience Level**: Casual viewers/players or experienced with the genre?
3. **Interests**: What do they already like? (Similar games, films, books, art styles)
4. **Values**: What matters to them? (Adventure, exploration, story, aesthetics, challenge)

### Part 2: Case Study Analysis

Find ONE example of a game, film, or animated project that targets a similar audience. Analyze it (200-250 words):
- Who is the target audience?
- How do the visuals reflect that audience?
- What specific design choices appeal to them?
- What can you learn from this example for your own project?

### Part 3: Design Implications

Write a short reflection (150-200 words) on how understanding your audience will influence your design decisions. Consider:
- Art style choices
- Level of detail
- Color palette
- Tone and atmosphere

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
It's okay to design for an audience that includes yourself! Many successful projects come from creators making something they personally want to see. Just be clear about who else might enjoy it.
{{< /callout >}}

## What to Turn In

Submit to Canvas:
1. Target audience definition (answers to Part 1 questions)
2. Case study analysis (200-250 words)
3. Design implications reflection (150-200 words)

## Grading Criteria

- **Audience Definition** (30%): Clear and specific
- **Case Study Analysis** (40%): Thoughtful analysis with specific examples
- **Design Implications** (30%): Shows understanding of audience impact on design
