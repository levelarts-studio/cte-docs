  Assignment 1.2 — Defining Your World's Environment
</div>

Now that you have a basic concept for your world, it's time to define the **physical environment** in detail. Where does your story take place? What does it look like, sound like, and feel like? This week focuses on the tangible, sensory aspects of your world.

## Physical Environment Elements

When defining an environment, consider:

### Geography & Landscape
- Terrain types (mountains, forests, deserts, oceans, urban, etc.)
- Climate and weather patterns
- Scale (small village, vast continent, single building, entire planet)
- Key landmarks or locations

### Visual Atmosphere
- Lighting (bright and sunny, dark and moody, magical glow)
- Color palette (warm, cool, saturated, muted)
- Architecture style if applicable
- Natural vs. built environment balance

### Sensory Details
- What would someone hear in this world?
- How does the air feel? (Humid, dry, cold, oppressive)
- What are the dominant textures? (Smooth metal, rough stone, soft organic)

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Environment Description

Write a detailed description of your world's primary environment (300-400 words). Include:

1. **Geography**: Describe the physical landscape
2. **Climate**: What's the weather like?
3. **Key Locations**: Identify 2-3 important places in this world
4. **Atmosphere**: How does it feel to be in this environment?

Use vivid, specific language that helps readers visualize your world.

### Part 2: Reference Board

Create a **digital mood board** (using Google Slides, Canva, or PowerPoint) with 6-8 reference images that capture the look and feel of your environment. Include:
- Real-world locations that inspire your setting
- Concept art from games or films with similar atmospheres
- Color palette examples
- Architectural or natural forms

Label each image with 1-2 sentences explaining why you chose it.

### Part 3: Environment Map (Sketch)

Create a simple hand-drawn or digital map showing:
- Major geographical features
- 3-5 key locations marked and labeled
- Scale indicator (optional but helpful)
- North/directional indicator

This doesn't need to be artistic—focus on clarity and layout.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
Your environment should support the story or scene you want to create. Think about how the setting can enhance the mood and narrative you're building.
{{< /callout >}}

## What to Turn In

Submit to Canvas:
1. Written environment description (300-400 words)
2. Reference mood board (digital file or link)
3. Environment map (photo of sketch or digital image)

## Grading Criteria

- **Environment Description** (40%): Detailed, vivid, and specific
- **Reference Board** (30%): Well-chosen images with clear explanations
- **Map** (30%): Clear layout showing key locations and geography

## Pro Tip

The environment you define here will be what you **model in Blender in Year 2** and **render in Unreal Engine in Year 3**. Choose something that excites you but is also achievable—think about modeling one specific location from your world rather than an entire continent.
