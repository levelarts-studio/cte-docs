  Assignment 1.3 — World History & Timeline
</div>

Every compelling world has a **history** that shapes its present. This week, you'll develop the backstory and timeline for your world. Understanding what came before helps you make informed decisions about what exists now—and why.

## Why History Matters in World-Building

History provides:
- **Context**: Why does your world look and function this way?
- **Conflict**: Past events create current tensions and story opportunities
- **Authenticity**: Worlds with history feel more real and lived-in
- **Design Justification**: Architectural ruins, artifacts, and technology levels all come from history

## Assignment Directions

### Part 1: Timeline of Major Events

Create a timeline of **5-10 significant events** in your world's history. For each event, include:
- **When it happened** (specific dates or "Ancient Era," "Recent Past," etc.)
- **What happened** (1-2 sentence description)
- **Impact** (How did this shape the world as it exists now?)

Format this as a visual timeline (Google Slides, Canva, or hand-drawn diagram).

### Part 2: Historical Deep Dive

Choose ONE event from your timeline and write a detailed account (250-300 words) exploring:
- What led up to this event?
- Who was involved?
- What were the consequences?
- How do people in your world remember this event today?

### Part 3: Artifacts and Evidence

Describe **2-3 physical artifacts or landmarks** that remain from historical events. These could be ruins, ancient technology, monuments, or natural formations created by past events.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Focus on events that **directly influence** the specific scene or story you want to tell in your capstone project.
{{< /callout >}}

## What to Turn In

Submit to Canvas:
1. Visual timeline with 5-10 major events
2. Historical deep dive on one event (250-300 words)
3. Description of 2-3 historical artifacts or landmarks

## Grading Criteria

- **Timeline** (35%): Clear, well-organized, shows cause and effect
- **Historical Deep Dive** (40%): Detailed, well-written, shows critical thinking
- **Artifacts** (25%): Creative and clearly connected to world history
