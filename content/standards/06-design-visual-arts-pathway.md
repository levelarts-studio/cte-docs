The **Design, Visual, & Media Arts Pathway Standards (17.0–20.0)** build on the foundational standards to incorporate technical skills, industry software workflows, and specialized creative production.

Our three-year curriculum centers on the **Game Design (GD)** specialization, with interdisciplinary modules drawn from **Animation & VFX (AV)** and **Design & Graphic Arts (DGA)**.

---

## 17.0 Animation & VFX (AV): Skill Building

- **AV.17.1 Art & Design Fundamentals** &mdash; Apply elements and principles of art and design to characters, props, and environments; differentiate lighting sources and types, and apply color theory to establish time, atmosphere and mood when lighting and shading a scene. [^9] &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.1/)

- **AV.17.2 Life Drawing, Anatomy & Perspective** &mdash; Create anatomically and physiologically accurate bipedal (human) and quadrupedal (animal) forms for use in animation; apply proportion, depth of field, anatomy, body mechanics, and facial expressions when designing characters. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.2/)

- **AV.17.3 Layouts & Environments** &mdash; Design layouts, environments, and backgrounds using principles of visual composition; refine setting, lighting, and depth to align with character scale, story, style, and animation requirements. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.3/)

- **AV.17.4 Character Design & Posing** &mdash; Use dynamic poses to develop turnarounds and action poses that convey emotion, gestures, personality and traits; generate multiple turntable and orthographic views including a range of character body types and costume designs. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.4/)

- **AV.17.5 Storyboarding** &mdash; Apply principles of composition, camera shots and angles (wide, medium, closeups, and extreme closeups), line work, and visual storytelling when developing storyboards for 2D and 3D animation. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.5/)

- **AV.17.6 Animation Types** &mdash; Compare different types of animation tools, workflows, visual styles, and industry applications (i.e., 2D, 3D, stop motion, and motion graphics). &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.6/)

- **AV.17.7 Animation Principles** &mdash; Apply the twelve principles of animation to characters, objects, and text (e.g., squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight ahead action and pose to pose, follow through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing, and appeal). &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.7/)

- **AV.17.8 3D Animation Fundamentals** &mdash; Design and model props, vehicles, and characters to demonstrate core animation principles (e.g., weight, timing, visual storytelling); develop and apply rigging systems to enable animation using keyframing and/or match moving.[^10] &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.8/)

- **AV.17.9 VFX Techniques** &mdash; Use VFX tools and techniques (i.e., rotoscoping, digital painting, and compositing) to isolate elements, enhance visuals, and seamlessly integrate layers within a scene; analyze emerging VFX technologies and workflows. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.17.9/)

## 18.0 Animation & VFX: Process and Practice

- **AV.18.1 Production Pipelines & Department Roles** &mdash; Evaluate the production pipeline, roles, and responsibility of each department, including the steps, skills, and processes within each pipeline stage (e.g., pre-production, production, and post-production). &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.1/)

- **AV.18.2 Storyboarding & Animatics** &mdash; Develop detailed storyboards and animatics that incorporate timing, camera framing, sound, and motion to plan scenes for both 2D and 3D animation. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.2/)

- **AV.18.3 Previsualization Techniques** &mdash; Convert animatics into detailed previsualizations by integrating characters, props, and environments to accurately plan timing, camera angles, and scene composition (rendered or in real-time). &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.3/)

- **AV.18.4 Animation Production** &mdash; Animate scenes using keyframes, frame-by-frame techniques, motion capture, or procedural animation; apply animation principles to both 2D and 3D characters and elements in multiple scenes. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.4/)

- **AV.18.5 Basic Effects** &mdash; Enhance scene realism and visual interest by creating and integrating dynamic simulations and digital effects in alignment with the show's style and aesthetic (e.g., particle systems, smoke, fire, water, fur/hair, and destruction). AV.18.6 Cinematic Lighting & Effects: Enhance realism and visual interest in animated scenes by integrating lighting effects, advanced shading, textures and material rendering techniques to define mood, simulate real-world surfaces, and support the story. AV.18.7 Rendering & Compositing: Render final sequences or export frames; composite multiple layers, effects, and color corrections to produce seamless, polished animations for both 2D and 3D projects. AV.18.8 Editorial Workflow: Collaborate with an editorial team to sequence shots, integrate sound design, voiceover, and music, apply color grading, and finalize the animation for distribution across platforms.[^11] &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.5/)

- **AV.18.9 Pipeline Collaboration & Project Management** &mdash; Coordinate workflows and communication among departments, utilize asset/version management systems, and follow project timelines and quality standards for both 2D and 3D animation productions. &nbsp;[View Aligned Modules &rarr;](/standards/av.18.9/)
