This site hosts all course content for the **Arts, Media & Entertainment (AME) Game Art & Design 3-Year Pathway** developed by Level Arts Studio. It provides comprehensive lesson plans, assignments, resources, and standards alignment for a progressive curriculum that takes students from initial concept development through professional portfolio production.

## Program Overview

The AME Game Art & Design pathway is structured as a **three-year journey** where students develop a single capstone project from concept to completion:

- **Year 1: Conceptualization & 2D Toolkit Mastery** - Establish narrative foundations and visual design using Krita, Canva, and Google Docs
- **Year 2: 3D Technical Modeling Core** - Master Blender for character, prop, and environment modeling with professional workflows
- **Year 3: Production, Integration & Professional Polish** - Integrate assets in Unreal Engine and build a portfolio-ready showcase

Each year builds upon the previous, with students applying new technical skills directly to their evolving capstone project. This **tool-first, apply-immediately** approach mirrors real game production pipelines.

## How to Use This Site

- **Year/Course Pages**: Each year has an overview page with learning objectives and software focus
- **Units**: Each course contains 6 units (18 total across three years). Unit pages include learning objectives, standards alignment, and weekly assignments
- **Assignments**: Each unit has 5 weekly assignments (90 total). Click assignment cards for detailed instructions, resources, and learning goals
- **Semester Projects**: Every 3 units includes a milestone project (6 total) that demonstrates cumulative learning
- **Resources**: Downloadable guides, templates, and references are available throughout
- **Standards Alignment**: All content maps to California AME CTE standards and AVG (Animation, Visual Effects, and Games) pathway benchmarks

## Capstone Project Philosophy

Students work on a **single project across all three years**:
- Year 1: Define the world, characters, and visual style
- Year 2: Model and texture all assets in 3D
- Year 3: Integrate, light, render, and showcase in a professional portfolio

This approach ensures deep engagement, iterative improvement, and a comprehensive portfolio piece that demonstrates mastery of the entire production pipeline.

## AI Use Policy

AI tools are part of modern creative work. You may use them here under clear guidelines:

| ✅ Allowed                                | ❌ Not Allowed                                      |
| :---------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| Brainstorming ideas or outlines           | Submitting AI-generated work as if it were your own |
| Research support and technical help       | Using AI to skip assignments or creative effort     |
| Generating inspiration or references      | Omitting credit when AI tools were used             |
| Editing support (spelling/grammar checks) | Using AI to bypass fundamental skill-building       |

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
If AI contributes, **you must cite it**. Example: *"Concept outline generated with ChatGPT, revised and developed by student."*
{{< /callout >}}

The goal is to develop **your own skills and artistic voice**. AI should assist your learning process, not replace it.

## Standards & Industry Alignment

This curriculum aligns with:
- **California Arts, Media & Entertainment (AME) CTE Framework (2024)**
- **Design, Visual, and Media Arts Pathway**
- **Animation, Visual Effects, and Games (AVG) Focus Area**

Students graduate with industry-recognized skills in concept art, 3D modeling, texturing, lighting, rendering, and portfolio development—preparing them for careers as concept artists, 3D modelers, environment artists, technical artists, and game designers.
